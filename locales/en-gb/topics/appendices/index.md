# Appendices

**Reference material for the people who run a metrics programme: terms, formulas, checklists, templates, a self-assessment, sources, and an index.**

The eleven parts before this one explain what to measure and how to use the measures without harm. The appendices are the tools to keep beside them. They do not follow the content topic template, because they are meant to be looked up, copied, and adapted, not read from start to finish.

Most of this material is assembled from the earlier topics. The glossary points each term to its home topic. The formulas reference, the consolidated sources, and the index are generated from the definitions, gaming tables, and reference lists in the topics, so the home topic is always the authority and these appendices are regenerated when a topic changes. The checklists, templates, and self-assessment condense advice from Part 11 into forms that a team can use in an hour.

## Topics in this part

- **12.1 Glossary**: terms and acronyms, each pointing to its home topic.
- **12.2 Metric definitions and formulas reference**: one entry per metric, with definition, formula, unit, data source, gaming vector, and guardrail.
- **12.3 Checklists**: metric review, dashboard launch, and programme audit.
- **12.4 Templates**: a metrics charter, a metric card, a dashboard specification, and a review agenda.
- **12.5 Maturity self-assessment**: the consolidated maturity model for a team to self-assess.
- **12.6 References and further reading**: consolidated references.
- **12.7 Index**: concepts, frameworks, and metrics by topic number.

## How these topics interrelate

The glossary (see topic 12.1, Glossary) and the index (see topic 12.7, Index) help a reader find the home topic for a term or a metric. The formulas reference (see topic 12.2, Metric definitions and formulas reference) is the place to look before adopting a metric, because every entry carries its gaming vector and guardrail, and the metric card template (see topic 12.4, Templates) is the form in which a team records its own variation of an entry.

The checklists (see topic 12.3, Checklists) and the self-assessment (see topic 12.5, Maturity self-assessment) are the two ways of reviewing a programme. The checklists review artefacts: a metric, a dashboard, a programme. The self-assessment reviews practice, in a conversation of an hour that produces one or two small next steps. Use the checklists to prepare for a review and the self-assessment to hold it. The sources (see topic 12.6, References and further reading) support both, and they carry a caution: books, papers, and standards were cited from memory and should be confirmed before formal use.
