# Glossary

Terms and acronyms used in this book, in alphabetical order. Each entry gives a short plain definition and points to the home topic, where the term is explained and used. Where this glossary and a home topic differ, the home topic is the authority.

## A

- **Alert quality**: how often alerts are actionable, timely, and worth waking someone for, as opposed to noisy or duplicated (see topic 5.5, Monitoring and alert quality metrics).
- **Automation**: software doing work that a person used to do, measured by what it removes and what it breaks (see topic 10.6, Automation and orchestration metrics).
- **Automation bias**: the tendency to accept machine output without enough scrutiny (see topic 10.1, AI in service management measurement).
- **Availability**: the share of time, or of requests, in which a service does what its users need (see topic 5.3, Availability metrics).

## B

- **Backlog**: the ordered list of work waiting to be done, whose age and health can be measured (see topic 2.6, Backlog and refinement metrics).
- **Baseline**: a recorded view of how things stand before a change, used for later comparison (see topic 10.1, AI in service management measurement).
- **Blameless review**: a review of an incident that looks at systems and decisions without assigning fault to individuals (see topic 4.2, Postmortem and learning metrics).
- **Bypass rate**: the share of production changes that did not pass through the delivery pipeline (see topic 10.7, Pipeline and automated change metrics).

## C

- **Calibration**: the agreement between the probability a forecast states and how often the event happens (see topic 10.9, Forecasting and predictive analytics metrics).
- **Change enablement**: the ITIL practice of making changes safely and quickly, with approval proportional to risk (see topic 4.3, Change enablement metrics).
- **Change failure rate**: the share of changes that cause degraded service or need remediation (see topic 4.4, Change failure and rework).
- **Charter (metrics)**: a short written statement of what measures are used for and never used for, signed by leaders (see topic 11.3, Rolling out metrics without breeding fear).
- **CMDB**: configuration management database, the store of configuration items and their relationships (see topic 7.4, Configuration and CMDB quality metrics).
- **Continual improvement**: the ongoing, small, evidence-based improvement of services and ways of working (see topic 9.5, Continual improvement metrics).
- **Continuous delivery**: the practice of keeping software and services always in a releasable state and delivering value in small, frequent, low-risk steps (see topic 2.8, DORA metrics).
- **Cycle time**: the elapsed time from when work starts to when it is finished (see topic 2.2, Lead time, cycle time, and flow efficiency).

## D

- **Dashboard**: a display of a small set of measures for a named audience, with trend and context (see topic 11.1, Designing a service metrics dashboard).
- **Data lineage**: the record of where data came from and what was done to it (see topic 10.8, Analytics and data quality metrics).
- **Data quality**: how complete, accurate, consistent, timely, and valid data is for the use made of it (see topic 10.8, Analytics and data quality metrics).
- **Deflection**: a contact avoided because the person found an answer or completed a task by self-service, to be read with verified resolution and return contacts (see topic 3.7, Self-service and automation metrics).
- **Deployment frequency**: how often a team puts changes into production (see topic 2.8, DORA metrics).
- **Digital product**: a software-based offering with its own lifecycle and owner (see topic 1.8, ITIL 5 measurement and value outcomes).
- **DORA metrics**: the four delivery performance measures popularized by the DevOps Research and Assessment program: deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate, and time to restore service (see topic 2.8, DORA metrics).
- **Drift**: gradual change in the data or behavior of a model or system away from what was tested (see topic 10.5, AI governance and risk metrics).

## E

- **Error budget**: the amount of unreliability a service may spend within its objective over a period (see topic 5.2, SLIs, SLOs, and error budgets).
- **Estate map**: a list of the services, digital products, platforms, and AI systems an organization runs, each with an owner (see topic 1.8, ITIL 5 measurement and value outcomes).
- **Experience level agreement (XLA)**: an agreement framed around the outcomes and experience users need, as a complement to SLAs (see topic 6.2, Experience level agreements).

## F

- **FinOps**: the practice of managing cloud and technology spend through shared accountability between engineering, finance, and the business (see topic 7.6, FinOps and cloud cost metrics).
- **First contact resolution**: the share of contacts resolved at the first contact, read with return contacts (see topic 3.1, Service desk metrics).
- **Flow efficiency**: the share of elapsed time in which work is actively being worked on, as opposed to waiting (see topic 2.2, Lead time, cycle time, and flow efficiency).
- **Forecast interval**: a range intended to contain the actual value with a stated probability (see topic 10.9, Forecasting and predictive analytics metrics).

## G

- **Gaming vector**: a way in which a metric can be made to look better without the underlying outcome improving (see topic 1.2, Goodhart's law and the psychology of metrics).
- **Goodhart's law**: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure (see topic 1.2, Goodhart's law and the psychology of metrics).
- **Guardrail metric**: a measure paired with another to detect distortion in the first, or a practice with the same purpose (see topic 1.3, Outcomes over output: choosing what to measure).

## H

- **Human in the loop**: a design in which a person reviews or approves an automated or AI action before it takes effect (see topic 10.6, Automation and orchestration metrics).

## I

- **Incident**: an unplanned interruption to a service or reduction in its quality (see topic 3.2, Incident volume and priority).
- **ITIL**: a body of guidance for IT service management, whose fourth edition frames work as a service value system (see topic 1.1, Why measure service management).
- **ITIL 5**: the version of ITIL announced by its owner PeopleCert in 2026; only statements the owner has published are treated as verified in this book (see topic 1.8, ITIL 5 measurement and value outcomes).

## K

- **Knowledge management**: the practice of capturing, sharing, and improving what people know, measured by use and usefulness (see topic 3.6, Knowledge management metrics).
- **Known error**: a problem with a documented root cause or workaround, tracked until fixed (see topic 4.6, Technical debt and known errors).

## L

- **Lead time**: the elapsed time from a request or commitment to its delivery (see topic 2.2, Lead time, cycle time, and flow efficiency).
- **Little's law**: the relationship that average time in a stable system equals average work in process divided by average throughput (see topic 2.3, Work in process and queueing).

## M

- **Major incident**: an incident with significant impact that needs a coordinated response (see topic 3.4, Major incident metrics).
- **Maturity model**: a description of stages through which a practice develops, used as a conversation tool and never as a rating (see topic 11.4, Maturity model for metrics programs).
- **Mean time to restore**: the average time to restore service after an incident, usually read with percentiles because averages hide long cases (see topic 3.3, Incident response and resolution time).
- **Metric card**: a short record of a measure's definition, formula, source, owner, limits, gaming vector, and guardrail (see topic 12.4, Templates).

## N

- **Net Promoter Score**: a survey-based measure of the willingness to recommend, to be read with other experience measures (see topic 6.1, Customer satisfaction metrics).

## O

- **Observability**: how well the internal state of a service can be understood from the signals it produces (see topic 10.10, Observability and telemetry metrics).
- **OKR**: objectives and key results, a framework for linking goals to measurable outcomes (see topic 1.7, Measurement frameworks).
- **OLA**: operational level agreement, an internal agreement between teams that supports a service level (see topic 5.1, SLA, OLA, and XLA metrics).
- **Orchestration**: coordination of several automated steps, often across tools, into one flow (see topic 10.6, Automation and orchestration metrics).
- **Outcome**: a change in the situation of people or the organization that matters, as opposed to the output produced (see topic 1.3, Outcomes over output: choosing what to measure).

## P

- **Percentile**: the value below which a given share of observations fall, such as the 85th percentile of a time measure (see topic 1.6, Statistical literacy for service metrics).
- **Pipeline**: the automated path that takes a change from a developer's work to a running service (see topic 10.7, Pipeline and automated change metrics).
- **Policy as code**: rules enforced by a pipeline or platform and written as software (see topic 10.7, Pipeline and automated change metrics).
- **Postmortem**: a written review of an incident for learning (see topic 4.2, Postmortem and learning metrics).
- **Problem**: the underlying cause of one or more incidents (see topic 4.1, Problem management metrics).
- **Prompt injection**: an attack in which instructions hidden in input manipulate a language model (see topic 10.5, AI governance and risk metrics).
- **Psychological safety**: a shared belief that a team is safe for speaking up about mistakes and problems (see topic 11.3, Rolling out metrics without breeding fear).

## R

- **Release and deployment**: the practices of making new or changed services available and moving changes into production (see topic 4.5, Release and deployment metrics).
- **Return on investment**: the net benefit of an investment relative to its cost, stated with its assumptions (see topic 7.7, Return on investment and business case).
- **Risk register**: a record of identified risks with owners, ratings, and actions (see topic 8.2, Risk and control metrics).
- **Run chart**: a chart of a measure over time with a median line (see topic 1.6, Statistical literacy for service metrics).

## S

- **Self-service**: the ability for users to find answers or complete requests without contacting a person (see topic 3.7, Self-service and automation metrics).
- **Service level agreement (SLA)**: an agreement between a provider and a customer on the level of service (see topic 5.1, SLA, OLA, and XLA metrics).
- **Service level indicator (SLI)**: a measured aspect of service quality, such as the share of successful requests (see topic 5.2, SLIs, SLOs, and error budgets).
- **Service level objective (SLO)**: a target for an SLI over a period (see topic 5.2, SLIs, SLOs, and error budgets).
- **Service request**: a formal request from a user for something to be provided, such as access or information (see topic 3.5, Service request fulfillment metrics).
- **Shadow IT**: technology used without the knowledge or approval of the responsible function, including AI tools adopted by staff (see topic 10.5, AI governance and risk metrics).
- **Sustainable pace**: a rate of work that people can maintain indefinitely without harm to their health or the quality of work (see topic 9.2, Satisfaction and well-being metrics).

## T

- **Technical debt**: the accumulated cost of shortcuts and deferred maintenance that slows future work (see topic 4.6, Technical debt and known errors).
- **Telemetry**: the logs, metrics, and traces a running service emits (see topic 10.10, Observability and telemetry metrics).
- **Throughput**: the number of items of work finished in a period (see topic 2.1, Flow measures).
- **Toil**: repetitive, manual, automatable operational work that grows with the service and has no lasting value (see topic 5.7, Toil and on-call metrics).
- **Total cost of ownership**: the full cost of acquiring, running, and leaving something over its life (see topic 11.2, Tooling landscape).

## U

- **Unit economics**: the cost and value of a service per unit of use, such as per active user or per transaction (see topic 7.5, Service cost and unit economics).
- **Unplanned work**: work that arrives without being planned, such as fixing incidents, which competes with planned work (see topic 2.9, Operational work and unplanned work).

## V

- **Value stream**: the sequence of steps from a request to the value delivered, including waits (see topic 2.4, Value stream metrics).
- **Verification log**: a record of each ITIL 5 statement used in plans, with the owner's source and the date it was read (see topic 1.8, ITIL 5 measurement and value outcomes).
- **Verified resolution**: a resolution confirmed by the user or by evidence that the problem did not return (see topic 10.3, AI agent and chatbot metrics).

## W

- **Work in process (WIP)**: the amount of work started and not yet finished (see topic 2.3, Work in process and queueing).
