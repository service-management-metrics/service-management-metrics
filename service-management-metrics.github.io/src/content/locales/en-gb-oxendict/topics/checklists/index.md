# Checklists

Three checklists for the programme team: one for reviewing a single metric, one for launching a dashboard, and one for auditing a metrics programme. Use them as prompts for a conversation with the teams, not as a form to be filled in and filed. Tailor them, and drop items that do not fit your context.

## Metric review checklist

Use this before a metric is adopted and at each periodic review (see topic 1.4, Metrics governance and ownership).

**Purpose**

- [ ] The metric answers a question that a named audience asked.
- [ ] The decision it should inform is written down.
- [ ] It measures an outcome, or it is clearly paired with one (see topic 1.3, Outcomes over output: choosing what to measure).

**Definition**

- [ ] A written definition, formula, unit, and data source exist.
- [ ] The clock or boundary rules are explicit (when counting starts, stops, and pauses).
- [ ] An owner is named, and a person to ask when the number looks wrong.
- [ ] Known limits of the data are recorded, including gaps and changes in how it was entered.

**Honesty**

- [ ] The most likely gaming vector is written down.
- [ ] A paired guardrail metric or practice is in place.
- [ ] The metric describes a team, service, or system, and never ranks individuals.
- [ ] Time measures show a median and a high percentile, with the sample size (see topic 1.6, Statistical literacy for service metrics).
- [ ] A baseline of several periods exists, so changes can be read against ordinary variation.

**Use**

- [ ] The team that does the work sees the number first and helped to define it.
- [ ] The last review led to a recorded decision, including a decision to wait.
- [ ] A condition for retiring the metric is written down.

## Dashboard launch checklist

Use this before a dashboard is shown to its audience (see topic 11.1, Designing a service metrics dashboard).

**Audience and content**

- [ ] The audience and its questions are written down, and the audience helped to review a sketch.
- [ ] The view has no more than about nine measures, with detail one step down.
- [ ] The set is balanced across flow, quality, reliability, experience, and cost.
- [ ] Every measure has a companion that guards against its likely distortion.

**Clarity**

- [ ] Every value shows trend and context, with events noted.
- [ ] Every measure has a definition, source, owner, refresh time, and known gaps within one click.
- [ ] Targets are shown as references, and any status colour follows a stated rule that allows for variation.
- [ ] Decoration that adds no information has been removed.

**Trust and operation**

- [ ] The charter has been agreed, and individual-level views are off (see topic 11.3, Rolling out metrics without breeding fear).
- [ ] Teams have seen their own data and had time to correct it.
- [ ] Data is pulled automatically, with freshness checks and reconciliation with the source (see topic 11.2, Tooling landscape).
- [ ] A named owner, a review date, and a challenge route exist.
- [ ] The reports this dashboard replaces have a retirement date.

## Programme audit checklist

Use this twice a year, with the teams, alongside the self-assessment (see topic 12.5, Maturity self-assessment).

**Purpose and trust**

- [ ] The programme has a sponsor and a written charter that leaders follow.
- [ ] Reported breaches of the charter were handled and the outcomes published without names.
- [ ] Team surveys and near-miss reports do not suggest rising fear.
- [ ] No league tables of teams or individuals exist anywhere in the organization's tools or slides.

**Measures and data**

- [ ] Each team can name the decisions its measures informed last quarter.
- [ ] Definitions are kept in a portable catalogue, with owners.
- [ ] Source coverage, pipeline reliability, and reconciliation are checked and visible.
- [ ] An export of definitions and history has been tested in the last year.

**Balance and breadth**

- [ ] Outcome measures sit beside activity measures in each view.
- [ ] AI-related measures follow baselines, sampling, and verified outcomes, not volume alone (see topic 10.4, Metric inflation and quality dilution).
- [ ] Automation and pipeline measures include failures, rollback, and bypass, and each automation has an owner (see topic 10.6, Automation and orchestration metrics, and topic 10.7, Pipeline and automated change metrics).
- [ ] Data quality and uncertainty are shown beside the numbers, and ITIL 5 claims are labelled verified or unconfirmed (see topic 10.8, Analytics and data quality metrics, and topic 1.8, ITIL 5 measurement and value outcomes).
- [ ] Cost, risk, and people measures are present where they matter, and none are used to rank individuals.

**Improvement**

- [ ] Improvement actions from the last review are completed or dropped with a reason.
- [ ] Measures and reports were retired in the last year.
- [ ] The effort asked of teams for measurement has been estimated and is acceptable to them.
- [ ] The programme itself has been reviewed with the teams, and the plan was changed as a result (see topic 11.5, An incremental adoption roadmap).
