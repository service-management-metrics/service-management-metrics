# Shared maturity model

Every content topic's `## Maturity model` section uses this same five-level scale, applied to that topic's subject. It is the same scale as the *Service Management Guide*, so the two books can be used together. Do not rename the levels or change their order.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Initiate** | The team recognizes the practice but has no consistent way of doing it. Work is ad hoc, depends on individuals, and is mostly undocumented. |
| 2 | **Develop** | Some teams have working practices, but they differ from team to team. Early tools, templates, and habits exist but are not shared or expected. |
| 3 | **Standardize** | A common, lightweight approach is written down and used across teams. Roles are clear. The approach is a starting point that teams may tailor with a stated reason. |
| 4 | **Manage** | Outcomes are measured and reviewed. Gaps lead to improvement work that is planned and resourced. |
| 5 | **Orchestrate** | The practice is coordinated across teams, suppliers, and customers, and is adapted continually as conditions change. Improvement is routine and comes from everyone, not only from a central group. |

## Applying it to a metric topic

A topic's maturity table has 3 to 5 rows. Each row is a sub-dimension of the topic. For example, for topic 3.3 (Incident response and resolution time), rows might be "definition and clocks," "data quality," and "use in improvement." Each cell is a short phrase specific to that row and level, not a repeat of this page.

For a metric topic the levels usually progress like this:

| Level | Typical state of the metric |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Initiate | Collected by hand or not at all; definitions vary by person. |
| 2 Develop | Collected by tool for some teams; definitions differ between teams. |
| 3 Standardize | One written definition, one data source, shown to the team that owns the work. |
| 4 Manage | Trended, paired with a guardrail metric, and reviewed on a cadence that leads to funded improvement. |
| 5 Orchestrate | Linked across teams and suppliers to outcomes, with definitions revised when they stop informing decisions. |

## Caveat

The scale is a conversation tool for teams, not a certification. It is not the same as the process maturity scales in ITIL, ISO/IEC 33000, or CMMI, and a team should not claim a score from those frameworks on the strength of this one. Part 11 (topic 11.4) applies the same scale to a metrics program as a whole.
